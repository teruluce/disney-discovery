PARK ETIQUETTE

Tokyo Disney Resort Etiquette: Park Rules & Japanese Customs

You do not need to know every Japanese custom to enjoy Tokyo Disneyland or Tokyo DisneySea. Start with a few practical habits: keep conversations considerate, join queues at the end, leave routes clear and follow cast members’ directions for shows and parades.

Tokyo Disney Resort is in Japan. Respecting local customs and the resort’s own rules helps everyone share the parks comfortably. Experience at another Disney resort can be useful, but its arrangements may not apply here. Take your cues from the signs and cast members at the place you are visiting.

Rules, directions and courtesy: The resort’s published rules set the requirements for a visit. Cast members give instructions for the conditions in each area. The everyday courtesy suggestions below are our practical advice; they are not a claim that every suggestion is a resort-wide prohibition.

In this guide

  • Conversation and phone calls
  • Queues and shared space
  • Sitting, standing and parade views
  • Asking for help and handling uncomfortable moments

Keep conversation comfortable for the people nearby

Use a conversational volume that suits the space, especially on the train to Maihama, in indoor areas and in close queues. Your group may be excited while the people next to you are resting, listening to an announcement or talking quietly.

  • Avoid shouting across a queue. Move closer to your group when it is safe to do so.
  • Keep calls off speakerphone, and use headphones for videos and music. Keep the volume low enough to hear instructions.
  • On trains, save phone conversations for after the journey. If something is urgent, send a message instead. See JNTO’s public-transport etiquette advice (French), which recommends quiet conversation and avoiding phone calls.

This is not a request to stay silent throughout a park day. Cheering during a performance, laughing and reacting naturally to an attraction are part of the fun. Adjust to the setting and avoid sustained noise that overwhelms other people’s experience.

Join the end of the queue and keep shared routes clear

Find the queue entrance before joining. If the end is unclear, ask a cast member rather than stepping into a gap. Do not cut in front of people or push through a line to reach friends. If someone needs to leave a queue or you become separated, ask a cast member how to handle it before trying to rejoin.

Pause out of the flow of traffic when checking the app, talking about your next stop or reorganising a bag. Keep entrances, exits, ramps and walkways clear. Give other guests room to pass, including people using wheelchairs or pushing strollers.

For the journey, see our Maihama train guide. For planning tools, use the official app guide; this page focuses on sharing the space rather than booking services.

Sitting on the ground is about location and instructions

There is a difference between sitting in a designated seated parade-viewing area and sitting in a passage that people need to use. Do not settle down in a walkway or block a doorway, even if you see an empty patch of ground. Look for a suitable resting place and ask a cast member if you are unsure.

We are not saying that all sitting on the ground is prohibited. For a parade or show, check whether your particular area is for sitting or standing and follow the directions given there. Do not assume that a spot is available for viewing just because someone else has stopped there.

Help the people behind you enjoy the show

The official English entertainment guidance asks guests to remove large hats or other headwear when watching performances. The Japanese guidance explains that guests may be asked to remove hats with large decorations or headbands depending on the show. Follow the directions for your performance. As an additional courtesy, check whether character ears or other accessories block the view behind you; this is not a park-wide ban on wearing headbands.

Stay seated in a seated viewing area and follow the instructions for standing areas. If a cast member asks you to change your position or remove something, follow that direction. Viewing arrangements can differ by location and performance; check the resort’s entertainment viewing guidance (Japanese) and ask on site if anything is unclear.

Keep phones and cameras from blocking other guests’ views. For equipment restrictions and photography considerations, read our Tokyo Disney Resort photography guide rather than assuming the equipment you used at another park is permitted here. For the nighttime parade itself, see our Electrical Parade Dreamlights guide.

Ask calmly, even if you do not speak Japanese

Try simple English, a short Japanese phrase or a translation app. Not every cast member will be fluent in English. Explain one thing at a time, show the relevant screen or location, and allow time for the person helping you to respond. Step aside from a busy entrance before opening a translation app.

  • すみません (sumimasen): “Excuse me” — a polite way to get someone’s attention.
  • ありがとうございます (arigatō gozaimasu): “Thank you.”

If you notice someone looking at you, a glance alone does not tell you their intention; you do not have to assume hostility. That does not mean dismissing an uncomfortable experience. If someone’s words or behaviour trouble you, or you need help, you can speak to a cast member and explain what happened.

Sources and current requirements

Official park and entertainment guidance checked on 30 October 2026. Requirements and viewing arrangements can change.

Use the official Theme Park Terms of Use (English) for the resort’s requirements, and the park visit guidance (Japanese) for further visitor information. Read current notices and follow cast member instructions on the day; a general etiquette guide cannot cover every local arrangement.

For the rest of your preparation, start with our first-time visitor guide and packing checklist. Small adjustments to volume, space and sightlines leave more room for everyone to enjoy the day.
